summary: 'We perform a systematic analysis of co-authorship networks of 273,367 climate change papers across the three IPCC Working Groups (WG1, WG2, WG3). Using network analysis and natural language processing methods, we quantify patterns of segregation and dependency among institutions over the last 25 years.'
abstract: 'Climate research co-authorship has become increasingly segregated by country income group. This trend is driven mainly by the growing concentration of collaborations among institutions in high-income countries, rather than by stronger collaboration among institutions in low- and middle-income countries. Despite this segregation, our results show that collaboration across institutions from different income groups is associated with substantially higher research productivity (up to 3.3 times more papers) and citation impact (up to 4 times more citations per paper) for institutions in both the Global North and the Global South. However, institutions that rely on cross-income collaboration as their primary mode of research production are less likely to lead publications as first authors and often occupy the periphery in the global climate research network. This pattern is more severe for Global South institutions, whose dependent engagement tends to coincide with Northern-led authorship rather than peer-level collaboration.'
